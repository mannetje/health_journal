# Add Fluid intake

## Why
Some people want to jot down what they drink during the day: because they were asked to keep a drinking list, because their huisarts or care team gave them a minimum or a maximum, or simply out of curiosity. Notes on paper or in a phone notes app are clumsy: every sip needs a number. The app has no place for it. This change serves two use cases:

1. **Log daily fluid intake.** The easiest possible log is a single tap on the drink, a cup of coffee, a glass of water, with the portions the person uses at home, and a daily total to look at afterwards.
2. **Set a minimum, a maximum or both.** The two are different needs, and a person may have either one, both or neither:
   - **A maximum**, because some people, due to a condition (for example heart failure or kidney disease), may not drink more than a set amount a day. The huisarts, cardiologist or hospital leaflet sets it per person, commonly around 1.5 to 2 litres.
   - **A minimum**, because some people need to reach a minimum intake, for example to prevent dehydration or because they were told to drink a set amount (Voedingscentrum gives a general minimum for adults: at least 1.4 litres a day from drinks).

   The numbers are personal and differ per person, so the app takes them from the person and only shows where the day's total stands against them. It never sets them.

## What Changes
- **Fluid tab on the Log screen** (its own tab, placed by the metric order) with **one button per drink**: Water, Coffee, Tea, Milk, Juice, Soup, Other (English; Dutch Water, Koffie, Thee, Melk, Sap, Soep, Overig). A count stepper (1 to 10, default 1) sets how many portions; a tap adds one entry of that drink at the current time with the amount count times that drink's own portion, in one step. A custom entry takes the drink, millilitres 10 to 5000, date and time (default now) and an optional 200-character comment. A plain line shows "Today: N ml" (the total of the local calendar day, all drinks counted equally), with the person's own numbers beside it when set. After a quick-add a short "Added 2 x Coffee, 250 ml, Undo" message lets the person take the tap back.
- **Look and feel, in the style of known hydration trackers:** large one-tap drink tiles with own-drawn icons, a **glass gauge** that fills (with a ripple, haptic tick and reduced-motion respect) showing today's total, a strip of small drink icons for today's entries, a calm Today line, a polished month calendar and charts, light and dark theme, phone, tablet and landscape. What the app deliberately leaves out compared with those apps: streaks, badges, mascots, points, challenges and motivational text, and any amount of its own. The glass is scaled to the person's own maximum (or minimum), with ticks for the numbers set; with none set it fills in steps of one glass and shows no scale. Patterns taken from the apps found during drafting: one-tap quick add with container presets, beverage types with icons, instant Undo, a monthly calendar with daily circles, a 7-day style chart, custom amount sheet.
- **Own portions:** each drink's portion (10 to 5000 ml) is editable and kept in preferences. Starting values: Water 250, Coffee 125, Tea 125, Milk 200, Juice 150, Soup 200, Other 200 ml. They are a convenience, not a recommendation.
- **The drink is a label.** It is stored with the entry and shown in History and in a per-drink chart; every drink counts equally towards the total (Voedingscentrum: all drinks count), and no caffeine, sugar, alcohol or nutrition framing is added. The list is fixed in version 1 (no own drinks, no alcohol entry).
- **Personal minimum and maximum (use case 2):** two independent optional daily numbers in ml (100 to 10000 each; when both are set the minimum must not be above the maximum), set only by the person, for example from the huisarts, a care team or a fluid restriction leaflet. A person may set only a maximum, only a minimum, both or neither. Both fields are empty until the person fills them; the app never suggests, prefills or recommends a number. With a number set, the Today line carries a plain position label in the form "name · range", like the other range labels (ADR 0005): "Below your minimum · 1400 ml", "Within your limits · 1400 to 2000 ml" or "Above your maximum · 2000 ml" (only the numbers that are set take part). The same label sits on each day-total row in History. Colours are the neutral sequential ramp, never traffic-light hues; no alert, notification or reminder is sent, and no advice wording exists. The person can change or clear either number at any time; this never alters stored entries.
- **Reference note ("About these amounts"):** an info control next to the limits opens a note that states what the Dutch sources say, as information with its source and not as a target of the app: Voedingscentrum's general amounts for adults (at least 1.4 litres a day from drinks for women, at least 1.4 to 1.8 litres for men, all drinks count, more in heat, sickness or exercise), that a maximum for a condition is set per person by the huisarts or cardiologist (hospital leaflets commonly mention 1.5 to 2 litres), and that the person asks their huisarts, with links to Voedingscentrum and Thuisarts. The note never prefills the fields and claims no endorsement.
- **History:** every tap is an entry; entries are listed in the merged timeline with edit and delete and, under the Fluid filter, a day-total row per day (with the position label when a number is set), plus a Fluid filter chip.
- **Graph view under the Fluid filter, like the Migraine view:** a Graph and Calendar toggle with the shared range chips. Graph: summary figures (average per logged day, highest day, days logged, and with limits set the number of days below the minimum, within the limits and above the maximum), a bar per day with up to two thin dashed lines at the person's minimum and maximum, a weekday chart, a time-of-day chart and a drinks chart (plain ml per drink). Calendar: a month grid where every day with entries shows its total in ml, shaded in one hue, with a small shape marker for the position against the person's numbers when set; tap a day to open it in History. Bars never change colour.
- **Export and backup:** CSV export and import (`timestamp,ml,drink,comment`; entries only) and, per profile in the `.hjbackup` document, a `fluidEntries` list plus `fluidMinMl` and `fluidMaxMl` (numbers or null). The drink portions are settings and are not in the backup. Entries carry their drink; files without one read as Other.
- **Notice:** the Profile notice says the app records what you enter, that a minimum or maximum shown is the person's own number, and that the app gives no advice.
- **All text in English and Dutch.**

## Impact
- **Database:** Room version bump with one migration that is the **next free number at implementation time (current database version + 1)**, so the change does not depend on the order in which features are built. It creates `fluid_intakes` (id, profile id, timestamp, ml, drink, nullable comment, index on profile and timestamp) and `fluid_settings` (profile id as primary key, nullable `min_ml`, nullable `max_ml`). Existing tables are untouched, so old data stays readable and an upgrade only adds empty tables. A migration test opens a database of the previous version with data.
- **Stored data shape:** the backup document gains `fluidEntries`, `fluidMinMl` and `fluidMaxMl` per profile; `schemaVersion` stays 1 (readers ignore unknown properties, older files restore with no entries and no limits). `docs/backup-format/` (README, JSON schema, `hjbackup.py`, test vectors, recovery page) is updated.
- **Preferences:** one portion size per drink only; no health entries and no minimum or maximum are kept in preferences.
- **Affected code:** `FluidIntake`, validation, daily grouping and statistics functions and commands with handlers in `:domain` (JVM tests including empty range, day boundaries, limits set, cleared and in each position); entities, DAOs, mapper, migration, repository methods and CSV adapters in `:data`; Log card with quick-add, size and limits settings, the reference note, History rows with day totals and the bar graph in `:app`.
- **Dependencies and permissions:** none. No notifications are added.
- **Docs:** README, CHANGELOG, `docs/dev/` pages, `docs/dev/zib-mapping.md`, screenshots (English and Dutch, light and dark), `docs/backup-format/`.

## Non-goals
- No target set or suggested by the app: no default, example or prefilled minimum or maximum, no "use the Voedingscentrum value" button, no personalised amount from age, sex, pregnancy or weather, no reminder or "drink more" nudge, no progress ring, bar fill or percentage toward an amount, and no advice, including when to drink or when to contact a care provider.
- No alert, notification, traffic-light colour or verdict wording ("enough", "too much", "too little", "dehydrated", "good", "bad"). Position labels only name where the total stands against the person's own number ("Below your minimum", "Within your limits", "Above your maximum"), always with the number.
- The minimum and maximum are not in the CSV (settings, not entries).
- No own drinks, no alcohol entry and no per-drink judgement; the drink list is fixed.
- No import from smart bottles, Bluetooth or Health Connect.
- No link to medication, the pillbox or any other metric, and no output (urine) recording.

## Open questions
- Compliance review of the glass gauge: it is a picture of the total against the person's own number (like the dashed lines and the position label), not a target of the app. It is the nearest the design comes to the progress ring that the compliance capability forbids, so it is called out for the spec review. Alternative: no gauge, only the icon strip and the numbers.
- Whether the fixed list of seven drinks is right, and whether soup belongs in it (Radboudumc counts soup towards a restriction; Voedingscentrum general amounts are "from drinks"). Soup is kept, and the reference note says what counts differs per care team.
- Dutch drink names (Water, Koffie, Thee, Melk, Sap, Soep, Overig), the limit labels ("Jouw minimum", "Jouw maximum", "Jouw grenzen") and the position labels ("Onder je minimum", "Binnen je grenzen", "Boven je maximum") are for the final wording review.
- Compliance review of the position labels: they compare the total with the person's own number, in the style of the NHG range labels, with neutral colours. It is the user's call whether that is acceptable or too close to a verdict (design section 5).
- Whether non-drink fluids (soup, yoghurt) are logged is left to the person; the app does not say what counts.
